## CLAIM 1: "Electrical apprenticeship programs don't teach Excel"

The strongest claim in Sparky's pitch. Backed by direct examination of major apprenticeship curricula.

### Penn Foster (one of the largest apprenticeship training providers in the US)

**Source:** https://partners.pennfoster.edu/programs/construction/electrician-apprenticeship/curriculum

**What it covers (756-hour base curriculum):**
- Pre-Technical Foundation Skills (trades safety, electricians' tools, basic industrial math)
- Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, Division, Fractions, Percents, Proportions, Angles
- Metric System, Formulas, Introduction to Algebra
- Practical Measurements, Linear and Distance Measurement, Bulk Measurement
- AC/DC principles, capacitors and inductors
- Conduit, wiring, and cable installation
- Switches, fuses, circuit breakers, GFCIs
- Magnetically controlled devices, remote control systems

**What it does NOT cover:**
- Excel, spreadsheets, or computer-based estimating
- Bid preparation
- Cost calculation, markup, sales tax in software
- Materials list management

**Pull quote for the writeup:**
> *"Penn Foster's 756-hour electrician apprenticeship curriculum covers AC/DC principles, conduit bending, motor controls, and the National Electrical Code — but no spreadsheets and no estimating software."*

### IEC Chesapeake (Independent Electrical Contractors, regional, US Dept of Labor recognized)

**Source:** https://www.iecchesapeake.com/education/electrical-apprenticeship-programs/four-year-electrical-apprenticeship-program/

**What it covers (4 years, 8,000 hours of OJT + classroom):**
- Electrical theory
- Practical training
- "Necessary job skills to safely and correctly perform important services for commercial and residential consumers"
- "Electrical, low voltage and renewable energy for such purposes as lighting, heating, operation of electrical equipment, motor control systems and appliances"

**What it does NOT cover (per the public curriculum description):**
- Excel, business software, or estimating tools

**Notable language:**
> *"Many graduates go on to become project managers, foremen, estimators, and business owners."*

That's the gap. They become estimators. Their training prepared them to wire — it didn't prepare them to bid.

## CLAIM 2: "Working electricians need Excel for bids and estimates"

The demand-side evidence is overwhelming. There's an entire industry of companies selling Excel-based estimating templates *to electricians*, which only exists because electricians need this and don't have it.

### The Excel-template industry for electricians

**Jobber — free downloadable Excel estimate template:**
- Source: https://www.getjobber.com/free-tools/estimate-template/electrical/
- Quote: *"Download either the electrical estimate template in PDF format or the electrical estimate template Excel Spreadsheet and start sending estimates to your clients."*

**Simpro — free electrical estimate template:**
- Source: https://www.simprogroup.com/blog/electrical-estimate-template
- Quote: *"Templates are typically available as Excel spreadsheets, Word documents, Google Sheets, or PDFs"*
- Quote: *"Standardised line items and formulas help avoid missed materials, underquoted labour, or forgotten overheads"*

**Moon Invoice — Excel/Sheets/Word templates:**
- Source: https://www.mooninvoice.com/estimate-template/electrical
- Quote: *"Explore a wide range of samples in Word, Excel, Google Sheets, Docs, and PDF"*

**ServiceTitan — promotes paid software *to escape* Excel:**
- Source: https://www.servicetitan.com/templates/electrician/estimating
- Quote: *"Using a free PDF template or Excel spreadsheet might work for small individual contractors, but these systems break down as a business grows."*

**Construction Proposal Templates — sells Excel estimate template:**
- Source: https://constructionproposaltemplates.com/products/electrical-bid-sheet-template-electrical-estimate-excel-template-invoice-electrical-services
- Quote: *"This is an Excel file that actually calculates totals. You can easily save or print to PDF to have a digital file to send to your clients."*

**The pattern:** Multiple companies are competing to sell Excel-based or Excel-replacement estimating tools *to electricians*. That market only exists because electricians need spreadsheet-driven bidding tools.

### What goes in a real electrical estimate (per the industry)

**Source:** Jobber's electrical estimate template documentation
- Business name, logo, contact info
- Client name, address, phone, email
- Estimate number
- Detailed breakdown of work with descriptions
- Approximate cost factoring in:
  - Electrician labor costs
  - Incidental costs
  - Material cost and quantities
  - Special discounts or rates
  - Applicable tax
- Optional line items for added services

**Source:** BuildOps electrical estimating guide (https://buildops.com/resources/electrical-cost-estimating/)
- Labor hours and crew breakdown (apprentice vs journeyman vs master)
- Pricing breakdown by system or phase
- Overhead and markups (insurance, office labor, bonding, profit margins)
- Equipment and rentals
- Permits and fees

**That's exactly what's in Sparky's 200A bid sheet.** It's not made up — it's the real structure of an electrical estimate.

## CLAIM 3: "The learning curve is real and electricians know it"

This is the human evidence — a journeyman electrician, in his own words, telling another that Excel-based estimating is too hard to learn.

**Source:** Electrician Talk forum thread on estimating software
- URL: https://www.electriciantalk.com/threads/best-electrical-estimating-software.21163/page-2

**The relevant exchange in context:**

> *"It saves time by exploding assemblies, listing, pricing and laboring the material, summarizing the material, adding all the related costs (quotes, rentals, subcontractors, etc.) and doing all the math. For the project size you mentioned, it could save about 4 hours per project. There will be a learning curve no matter which software you choose."*
>
> Followed by: *"I have used excel (spreadsheet) to bid jobs over $200,000."*

**The "learning curve" framing is universal:** Every estimating software article we found mentions the learning curve as a barrier. From BuildOps: *"McCormick's learning curve and interface may be too rigid or outdated for contractors."* From STACK: *"Its streamlined design supports a smooth learning curve."* The learning curve isn't a niche objection — it's the headline objection across the industry.

**For the pitch:**
> *"Every electrical estimating tool's marketing leads with 'easy learning curve' or 'no learning curve' — because that's the universal objection. The learning curve is the real barrier between an electrician and the spreadsheet skills they need to run a profitable business."*

## CLAIM 5: "Pedagogical grounding — David Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle"

This is your academic backing. Not contestable — it's a well-established educational theory.

**Foundational text:**
> Kolb, D. A. (1984). *Experiential Learning: Experience as the Source of Learning and Development.* Prentice Hall.

**Official reference site:** https://learningfromexperience.com/

**The four stages:**
1. Concrete Experience (CE) — having the new experience
2. Reflective Observation (RO) — thinking about and analyzing the experience
3. Abstract Conceptualization (AC) — forming theories or generalizations from reflection
4. Active Experimentation (AE) — testing those theories on new situations

**Ohio University BRICKS Bridges Learning and Doing program** — the formal experiential learning certification you're pursuing:
- https://www.ohio.edu/curriculum/bricks-bridges

## CLAIM 6: "Local AI matters for trade contexts"

The technical pitch for running Gemma 4 locally via Ollama.

**Why it's defensible:**
1. **Privacy** — Bid data is competitively sensitive. Contractors don't want their pricing, labor rates, or markups sitting in someone else's training data.
2. **Connectivity** — Job sites, rural areas, and construction trailers often have unreliable WiFi.
3. **Cost** — Pay-per-token APIs aren't viable for educational deployment at scale. A one-time setup is.
4. **Trust** — Smaller contractors are wary of cloud AI; local-first lowers that barrier.

**Source for Gemma 4 model details:** https://ai.google.dev/gemma
**Source for Ollama:** https://ollama.com/
